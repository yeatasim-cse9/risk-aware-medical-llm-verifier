## Candidate method: Paired Intervention Risk Control (PIRC; working name, not a novelty assertion)
For a question and original answer, construct evidence-linked features for original and proposed revision, including directional support/contradiction, missing evidence, evidence perturbation response, change magnitude and uncertainty. Fit risk and repair scores using train only; use parent/source groups for all splits. Learn a fixed small family of accept/revise/abstain policies from train/tune data. Freeze generator, revision, verifier, evidence corpus, inference parameters, parser and policy family before calibration. No gold answer in prompts, retrieval queries or features. Never call model agreement clinical truth.
A candidate algorithm is a paired repair-benefit score with preservation penalty and evidence sensitivity; compare to calibrated single-score accept/abstain and simple threshold revise cascades. The exact learned scoring function remains to be developed and checked against literature. The initial prototype only implements calibration of a fixed policy family. No new theorem claimed.

For reference-scored closed QA only, select one question per independent source group by a prelabel rule, with fixed original generation and revision. Let A indicate release (accept or revise), Y0 original correctness, Y1 final correctness (observed for released outputs). Report all denominators. Three constraints:
- release error risk R=Pr(Y1=0 | A=1);
- introduced error I=Pr(A=1,Y1=0 | Y0=1);
- useful coverage C=Pr(A=1).
Also report correct-to-wrong, wrong-to-correct, original-correct abstentions, overall correct released rate, repair among original errors, costs, and conditional metrics without hiding abstentions. Correction and harm are benchmark-defined, not measured patient outcomes. A fixed family of K policies and three one-sided exact binomial bounds with Bonferroni alpha/(3K) is valid under iid calibration source representatives from the target distribution and fixed policies independent of calibration. Conditional sample counts may be random; conditional Bernoulli bounds rely on that model. Zero denominator cannot certify risk. Choose maximum empirical coverage among certified policies with deterministic ties; if none passes, report NO_CERTIFIED_POLICY, no tuning after calibration or test. Illustrative prospective caps R<=0.10, I<=0.05, C>=0.50, alpha=.05, not clinical thresholds. Predefine sensitivity caps and adjust multiplicity before use; do not silently relax. Distribution shift, model dependence across source groups and selection into benchmark can invalidate guarantee. Report out-of-distribution empirical performance without transferring guarantee. For MedHallu paired roles, separate detection experiment only, not Y1 gold for free-text revision.

## Statistical analysis and release criteria
Freeze task definitions, source grouping, input availability, labels, model/quantization, retrieval snapshot, policy candidates, seeds, comparators, primary endpoints and analysis code before final test. Pilot only on train/tune, estimate group counts and variance, simulate paired minimum detectable effect and calibration feasibility, report uncertainty and do not assume 887 parents are independent. Specify smallest effect of interest prospectively (candidate 0.02 absolute AUPRC, justify or revise pretest). Use source-group clustered paired bootstrap (10,000 deterministic replicates; report invalid replicates), confidence intervals for effect sizes, Holm across prespecified confirmatory comparisons. Use group representative analysis for exact risk certification; full paired/group weighted analyses descriptive or cluster-aware separately. Report AUROC/AUPRC, macro-F1, per-class, Brier, reliability diagrams with fixed bins, risk-coverage/AURC, coverage at targets, subgroup denominators, uncertainty, and all seeds. Avoid claiming a guarantee from a bootstrap interval or treating seeds, paired answers, claims or windows as independent units. Negative results and failures retained. No test-set-driven policy selection, threshold search, model choice, data removal or repeated test runs after seeing results. Test once per frozen protocol; amendments versioned and exploratory unless fresh test data available.
 
## Nine evidence gates for aspirational top-tier standard
1. Problem importance: clear intended computational use, benchmark error definition and falsifiable hypotheses; no deployment claim.
2. Engineering: immutable data/code/model/env pins, independent aggregate/reproduction checks, failure handling, compute budgets and reproducible tables.
3. Data integrity: freeze source groups, audit provenance and semantic screening limits, shortcut controls, pretraining contamination limits, no private export.
4. Novelty: systematic nearest-work matrix; precise mathematical/algorithmic difference; strong matched baselines and ablations demonstrating benefit, or report no gain.
5. Statistics: prospective endpoint hierarchy, source-aware uncertainty, calibration feasibility/power, multiplicity, locked analysis, report negative outcomes.
6. Clinical benchmark validity: published expert labels with rights, explicit scope/label provenance and uncertainty; no newly human-certified labels or real-world safety assertion.
7. External validation: at least two task-appropriate held-out evaluations where feasible, multiple model families and shift tests; no claims of independence when shared sources or training exposure.
8. Actual experimental evidence: real authorized runs, validated artifacts, effect sizes and reproducibility; synthetic tests not effectiveness evidence.
9. Submission readiness: manuscript/appendix, auditable references and limitations, TRIPOD-LLM applicable checklist, AI disclosure, academic approvals and journal-specific scope/ranking check. No guarantee of acceptance or top-percentile rank.
 
All nine are targets, not achieved scores. No fabricated human approvals, new GPU runs or automatic readiness change. Prior MASTER 01D remains historical.
